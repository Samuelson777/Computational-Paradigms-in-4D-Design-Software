Executive Abstract

Four-dimensional (4D) design software encompasses computational environments that integrate temporal dynamics (t) directly into three-dimensional (x, y, z) geometric and semantic representations. Across contemporary engineering and materials science, 4D software bifurcates into two distinct yet mathematically analogous domains: macro-scale spatial-temporal construction simulation via 4D Building Information Modeling (4D BIM) in the Architecture, Engineering, Construction, and Operations (AECO) sector, and micro-to-meso-scale chemo-mechanical morphogenesis via 4D printing (4DP) inverse design software in advanced additive manufacturing.

This study by SAMUELSON G presents an exhaustive technical synthesis and empirical evaluation of both paradigms. In the AECO domain, the analysis examines OpenBIM Industry Foundation Classes (IFC) temporal schemas (IfcWorkSchedule, IfcTask, IfcRelAssignsToProcess), spatial-temporal workspace clash detection algorithms, and constraint-driven generative scheduling engines. Empirical benchmarks from multi-storey reinforced concrete, modular prefabrication, and subterranean pipeline projects demonstrate that integrated 3D+4D+5D BIM workflows elevate baseline composite accuracy from 78.4% (manual methods) to 96.7%, compress post-change re-estimation turnaround times by 97.1% (from 42.0 hours to 1.2 hours), reduce modular site durations by 50% (from 18 days to 9 days), and eliminate up to 100% of spatial-temporal pipeline collisions.

In the additive manufacturing domain, the analysis investigates the transition from forward kinematic self-assembly simulators (such as Project Cyborg) to deep learning inverse design architectures—specifically Fully Convolutional Networks (FCNs), normalizing flows (4DYNAMO), and semantic segmentation networks—that map target 3D deformed morphologies directly into fabrication-ready voxel material layouts and liquid crystal elastomer (LCE) bilayer orientations in a single non-iterative inference pass. Finally, emerging intellectual property frameworks governing AI-generated 4D transformations and G-code manufacturing instructions are evaluated under contemporary patent jurisprudence.

Taxonomic Foundations of Four-Dimensional Design Computation

Unifying the mathematical formulation of spatial-temporal occupancy in AECO with continuous chemo-mechanical morphogenesis in smart materials.

Traditional Computer-Aided Design (CAD) and static 3D modeling treat physical artifacts and built facilities as time-invariant Euclidean geometries. The introduction of the fourth dimension—time—fundamentally alters the computational representation of an object from a static boundary representation (B-Rep) or voxel mesh into a time-parameterized state function:

\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{x}, t), \quad \text{where } \mathbf{x} \in \mathbb{R}^3 \text{ and } t \in [t_0, t_f]

Despite operating at vastly different physical scales (10^4\text{ m} in civil infrastructure vs. 10^{-6}\text{ m} in micro-printed metamaterials), both paradigms solve an identical core problem: predicting, optimizing, and controlling how physical matter occupies, traverses, and transforms within three-dimensional space over time.
